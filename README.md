# Grocery Practice

**Version 0.0.36**

A 2D grocery store manager simulator for practicing the real job: receiving the night truck, building floats, working freight, keeping the shelves full, running the crew, and ordering for tomorrow. All without blowing the labor budget.

## Play

It's plain HTML/JavaScript, so there's no build step.

- **Locally:** serve the folder with any static server, e.g. `npx http-server -p 8123`, then open http://localhost:8123
- **Online:** play it at https://therarefew-arch.github.io/GroceryPractice/ (GitHub Pages, updates with every push to main).

**Controls:** `WASD`/arrows move · `Shift` run · `E`/`Space` interact (hold to repeat) · `Q` second action · mouse wheel, `+`/`-` or `Z` zoom · `M` sound · `P` pause · `1` `2` `3` speed · `Esc` leave the computer

**Gamepad:** left stick/d-pad move (the stick is analog) · `A` interact (hold to repeat) · `X` second action · `B` or `RT` run · `LB`/`RB` zoom · `Start` pause

**Extras:** hover over a crew member to see what they are working on · chain sorts and stocks within 6 seconds for a combo XP bonus · a minimap appears while zoomed in · synth sound effects (mute with `M`) · confetti on level-ups and finishing the truck · rush-hour warning at 4 PM

## The daily loop

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 5:00 AM | Last night's truck is in **Receiving**. Break mixed pallets down onto department **floats**. |
| Morning | Push floats to their aisle and work them onto the shelf. Overflow goes on that aisle's **bird** in Backstock. |
| 7:00 AM | Doors open. Every empty shelf is a lost sale and an unhappy customer. |
| All day | Check in **DSD vendors** at the back door, hang **price tags**, fix **scan errors**, fill holes. Every shopper leaves the aisles messier and some leave **go-backs**. |
| 5:00 PM | **Recovery** before the dinner rush: face every aisle and clear the go-backs. |
| Office | The computer has **Reports, Email, Crew & Schedule, Hiring, Display Planner, Forecast & Order**. |
| 9 PM–close | **Close recovery**: face every aisle again and clear the go-back cart. |
| 11:00 PM | Closing report with a letter grade, XP, a manager bonus, badges and coaching notes. |
| Night | On truck nights the truck you ordered arrives. Pull pallets off the trailer, or pay overtime to have it done. |

**Weeks and trucks:** store weeks run Saturday to Friday (Day 1 is a Saturday). Weekends are busier, and forecasts use a day-of-week factor. Trucks come every night with different loads (`TRUCK_SCHEDULE`: Sat LV/Frozen/Dairy, Sun HV, Mon HV/Frozen/Dairy, Tue KeHe/LV/HV, Wed Dairy, Thu KeHe/LV/HV/Frozen/Dairy, Fri KeHe/HV). You can only order departments that are on tonight's trucks, and each order has to cover the days until that department's next truck. The order window closes at `ORDER_CUTOFF` (2:00 PM); miss it and the system order goes.

**Daily checklist (📋):** the real floor-board routine, checked automatically: water and paper runs before open, backstock bird runs by day, today's cycle counts, closing last night's invoices (claim any shorts first), tagging holes left by warehouse cuts by noon, recall pulls and certification when a recall comes in, the planogram reset every other Tuesday, Monday ad planning, the Tuesday ad flip and Wednesday verify, the morning huddle before 8 AM on days you open, endcaps at least half full by 5 PM, answering time-off requests, performance reviews when they come due, Fri–Sun blitzes, the 5 PM recovery (every aisle 85%+ faced by 6 PM), the 6 PM dairy hole check, the 10–11 PM close run, close recovery and go-backs worked at 10:50 PM, reclaim worked by close, a clean backroom, the out-of-stock count, and one stretch goal. Find it on the office computer → Today.

**Your shift:** you're on the schedule too (the **You** row in 🗓 Schedule; by default you open 5a–2p, off Tuesday and Wednesday). You clock in when your shift starts. When it ends you're asked whether to go home; stay as long as you like, then use the 🏠 button or the staff door. While you're off the clock the crew runs the store on their own and time moves fast (an hour a second): watch, **Skip ahead**, **Come in now**, or **Log in from home** to use the computer (the order, forecast and schedule still have deadlines). The crew covers what can't wait for you: someone in the back checks in DSD vendors if there's no DSD Receiver, anyone cleans up a spill, someone pulls product from a failed cooler, and when their own work runs out they hang price tags, put away birds, fill holes, sweep and face. A ◆ Lead on shift settles arguments while you're gone; without one, nobody does. Store managers are salaried, so your shifts count in full on the GR-Mgr line of the Labor Demand report.

**Shifts:** Open 5a–2p, Truck 6a/7a/8a, Mid 10a–7p, Close 2p–11p, Night 11p–5a. The store closes at 11 PM. Shifts over 6 hours take a 1-hour unpaid lunch break.

**Hard mode by default:** cases show only the item name, so you need to know which aisle it belongs in. Turn on easy mode in Help & Settings to show the aisle.

**Grading:** sales vs goal (30), in-stock % (25), labor % of sales ≤ 12% (20), truck worked by noon (15), price integrity (5), DSD check-ins (5).

**Crew roles:** Truck Breakdown, Freight Pusher, Replenishment, DSD Receiver, Scan/Price Clerk, HBC Clerk, Dairy Clerk, Frozen Clerk, Night Unloader. Each has a shift, a skill level and a wage.

**Training & coaching:** new hires make realistic mistakes (wrong float, cardboard left in an aisle, tag on the wrong item, signing for a short DSD delivery, miscounting a cycle count, phone breaks). A red **!** appears over them. Walk up and press `E` to coach within 2 hours, or the mistake becomes a habit. Crew rank up Rookie → Trained → Pro → ★ Superstar (85% trained, coached 8+ times, 3+ days on the job). Superstars work 15% faster, make no mistakes and mentor rookies on their shift. Hover any worker to see their training bars.

**Morale & conflicts:** every worker has a morale level and a personality (Easygoing, Hothead, Perfectionist, Social, Quiet). Morale changes their work speed and how often they call out. Thank people for good work (walk up and press `E`, once a day each) and keep schedules steady. Arguments break out (orange **!!**): walk over and press `E` to talk it out, split them up, write up, send home or let go. The right call depends on how serious it is and the person's write-up history, and you always get the why. Below 20% morale, people give 3 days' notice; talk to them to change their mind. When someone quits, HR sends you their exit interview: why they left and what to fix.

**Promotions:** Pros and Superstars with 5+ days can be promoted to ◆ Lead (+$1.50/hr, max 2). Leads work faster, lift morale on their shift and calm arguments. Strong workers will ask to move up; answer within 3 days or they feel passed over.

**Special Item Requests:** customers ask for items you don't carry (email + 🛒 Special Items tab). Each department has room for 8 items, so carrying one means deactivating one: check the 7-day sales and drop the slowest. Answer within 3 days or the customer is upset.

**Dated dairy:** dairy ages on the shelf, and over-ordering makes it age faster. Do the code check every morning (E at the dairy shelf; it's on the daily list by 10 AM): pull expired product, mark down short-dated (sells first at 50% off) and rotate the oldest to the front. Customers who find expired product complain. A Dairy Clerk on shift does the check at 9 AM and 3 PM.

**Store events:** spills (clean within 30 minutes or a customer slips), cooler breakdowns (pull the product to the backroom cooler and call maintenance, or it spoils every hour), announced DM store walks and unannounced health inspections. Every finding comes with the why.

**Seasons:** every other week brings a seasonal event (Summer Cookout, Back to School, Big Game Weekend, Thanksgiving, Holiday Season) that lifts certain departments. The DM warns you the Monday before, and the suggested order does NOT include it, so adjust your orders yourself.

**Labor (Monday forecast, schedule, Labor Demand Exception):** every Monday, forecast next week's sales by day before 5 PM (Office computer → 📑 Labor). The system forecast doesn't know about seasons or the ad, so adjust it. Your forecast earns next week's labor demand: truck cases, sales, DSD vendors, price tags and the ad flip, by role. Write next week's schedule day by day in 🗓 Schedule and post it by Thursday 5 PM; changes after posting cost morale. Everyone works 5 days by default; over 40 paid hours is overtime (1.5x). The Labor Demand Exception report shows demand vs scheduled hours by role and day (GR-Dairy, GR-DSD Receiving, GR-Frozen, GR-HV-LV-DSD, GR-HV-LV-DSD-RPLN, GR-Liquor, GR-Scan Price, then weekly-only roles), with exceptions in bold. The DM grades your forecast accuracy and your schedule against demand.

**Backroom upkeep:** every broken-down pallet leaves an empty on its receiving spot: jack it (E) and stack it in RELOAD STAGING (bottom-left, 8 slots, 15 empties a stack). Every case leaves cardboard in the baler; when it's full, make a bale (E at the baler) or cardboard ends up on the floor. Trucks take reloads at night only if they have room: last stop on the route takes everything, a middle stop takes a little, the first stop takes nothing (the morning email tells you tonight's stops). Load reloads at the dock after the trailer is empty; a Night Unloader does it for you. Sweep the backroom mid-day and after 9 PM. Straighten crooked floats (they slow the breakdown). Each aisle's bird holds 14 cases of backstock; past that it overflows onto the sales floor, blocks the aisle and costs satisfaction every hour.

**Backstock birds (they have wheels):** each aisle's backstock lives on its bird, a 2-tier cart parked in BACKSTOCK (top middle). `E` at a bird pushes it; `Q` carries 3 cases instead. Push it to its own aisle and press `E` at the shelf to work backstock straight on, then park it back on its spot. A bird left on the sales floor counts as a blocked aisle until somebody puts it away (the crew does after 20 minutes). Replenishment, and Freight Pushers once the truck is out, run each aisle's bird on its backstock day; department clerks run their own. You can also order anyone to **Run the bird**. Breakdown and Night Unloader crew stack empties, bale and sweep when they have nothing better to do.

**Facing and go-backs:** every aisle has a faced level, shown as a bar under its sign (green 85%+, amber, red under 70%). Every customer who shops an aisle leaves it a little messier, and working freight fronts it a little. Below 70% faced, product pushed to the back looks like a hole: some customers walk past it (a "visual out": a lost sale even though the shelf has product), and a messy aisle costs satisfaction every hour. Press `E` at any shelf to face it (it keeps going until the aisle is 100% or you move). Run recovery at 5 PM and again before close. Crew on shift do it on their own from 5 to 6 PM and from 9 PM to close, and face the messiest aisle when their own work runs out, so schedule closers or it doesn't happen. **Go-backs** are things customers changed their minds about: most land on the go-back cart at the front end (between the registers), some get dumped on the wrong shelf (blinking boxes on the floor). Grab up to 12 from the cart or pick up strays, then `E` at each one's own shelf to put them back. Cold go-backs (dairy, frozen) are thrown away after 30 minutes out of the cooler, so work them first. The DM walk, inspections, closing report and weekly review all look at facing and go-backs, and there's a **Faced Up** badge for every aisle 90%+ at close with no go-backs left.

**On-hand accuracy and cycle counts:** the system keeps its own on-hand for every department: what came in on the invoices, minus what scanned at the register. The building doesn't always agree. Product walks out unscanned (theft is highest in Health & Beauty and Snacks & Beverage), the warehouse now and then ships a case of the wrong department, and a crew member who signs for a short DSD delivery gives the system cases you never got. The system never hears about any of it, so its on-hand drifts, and you inherit a store that hasn't been counted in a while. The suggested order (and the system order if you miss the cutoff) uses the **system** on-hand: phantom inventory (the system thinks you have it, you don't) means it orders too little and you run out, and the other way it over-orders. Every morning the system puts 2 departments on the count list (🔢 Counts on the office computer, and the daily checklist): first any department where customers found the shelf *and* its bird empty while the system still showed stock, then whatever was counted longest ago. To count one, press `E` at its shelf and `E` at its bird in Backstock (either order; a clipboard marks what's left). The system takes your count and tells you the variance; missing product is booked as unknown shrink. A Scan/Price Clerk works the list on their own, department clerks count their own department, and you can order anyone to count. Rookies sometimes miscount (a new **Counting** training area: coach them). The order screen marks departments not counted in a week and likely phantoms.

**Damages and reclaim:** some cases come in crushed on the truck. You find out when you break the pallet down: the good units keep going and the damaged ones land on the floor. Customers knock product off the shelf too, and a cleaned-up spill leaves the broken product behind. Damaged product on the sales floor costs satisfaction every hour. Take it to **RECLAIM** (the striped bin in the backroom by the office door): `E` to pick damages up (you can carry 8), `E` at the bin to put them in, and `E` again to scan the bin out. Today's truck damage scanned the same day is a **damage claim** on the invoice: full cost back. Other dry damages get 50% reclaim credit, and cold damages are thrown away. DSD vendors' own product isn't scanned: it waits in the bin, and the vendor credits it in full when you check them in, but only what's in the bin. Until it's scanned out, the system still counts damaged product as sellable, so unworked reclaim turns into phantom on-hand. Idle crew pick up damages, Breakdown and Night Unloader crew clear truck damage, and a Scan/Price Clerk scans the bin (without one, someone scans it late in the day so the claims don't expire). Reclaim left for days smells and draws pests: the DM and the health inspector both check it. The office computer's Today tab shows the bin and today's credits, and there's a **Claims Clerk** badge.

**Warehouse cuts and shorts:** you don't always get what you order. When the warehouse is out of an item, every case of it on your order is **cut**, and the invoice only bills what shipped. Most outs come on the morning **Warehouse bulletin** email from the Distribution Center, with a date it's expected back (dates slip). Some you only find out about on the **cut report**, which comes in when the trucks leave the warehouse. A few cases get cut at selection on any night, and now and then an item goes **on allocation**: at most a few cases per store per order, and anything over that is cut. The Forecast & Order screen lists what's out or limited for tonight's departments and shows what each department will really ship ("ships ≈"). The suggested order doesn't know about cuts. When an item is out, its customers buy the rest of the department, so order that up. A cut item leaves a **hole** on the shelf: customers who wanted it either switch to another item or walk out without it (that doesn't count against your in-stock). Hang a **"temporarily out" tag** on the hole (`E` at the shelf; a Scan/Price Clerk does it too): customers see it's the warehouse, not you, and more of them switch. When the item is back on the shelf, pull the tag. **Shorts** are different: the case is on the invoice but not on the truck. You find them once the truck is worked: office computer → Today shows the short, and you file a **shortage claim** before you close that invoice. Close it without claiming, and you paid for product that never came and the system counts it as on hand. There's a **Short Stop** badge.

**Recalls:** about once a week a recall notice comes in from corporate during the day: one item, certain lot codes, and the reason (a germ, an allergen that isn't on the label, pieces of plastic, the wrong product in the package). The register blocks the item right away, so every customer who brings one up gets turned away upset until it's off the shelf. Pull it from the **shelf** first (`E` at the department's shelf; this also posts the recall notice where it was): a **Class I** recall has to be off the shelf within 2 hours, **Class II** within 4. Then pull it from **backstock** (`E` at that aisle's bird) and from any **freight** still on pallets or floats (`E` at them). A red **!** marks each place that still has some. A recalled case that gets worked to the shelf before anyone pulls it is back on the shelf. Then **certify** the recall on the office computer (Today) by close the next day: the pulled units are scanned out and credited in full. Until then the system still counts them as on hand. Certify before everything's pulled and the recalled product is still on your shelf with your signature on the form; rookies sometimes miss the back of the shelf, so walk it before you sign. The warehouse pulls those lots too, so the item is cut from your orders for a few days; take the recall notice down when it's back. A Scan/Price Clerk works recalls before anything else, department clerks pull their own department, idle crew help, and you can order anyone to "Pull the recall". The DM and the health inspector both check for recalled product on the shelf. There's a **Recall Ready** badge.

**Planogram resets (every other Tuesday):** the Saturday before, Space Planning emails a reset packet for one aisle (they rotate): 2 discontinued items, 2 new items, how many items move, and how many new shelf labels. Discontinued items stop shipping that day (they're cut from your orders), so sell them down; whatever is left gets pulled during the reset and goes to clearance at half price. The new items' first cases come on Monday night's truck. Monday's checklist reminds you to plan the hours. On Tuesday, work the reset at that aisle's shelf (`E`, hold it): about 2½–3 hours of work (pull the discontinued items, move everything to its new spot, set the new items). A department clerk for that aisle works it, idle crew help, and you can order anyone to "Planogram reset". Once the set is done, hang the new labels (they're price tags in that aisle; a Scan/Price Clerk hangs them). While the aisle is torn up it looks empty to customers, so start before the doors open and finish fast. It's due by close Tuesday; when the work and the labels are done, the new items are live, selling and in your orders. A late reset gets a daily email from the DM and shows up on the DM walk. The office computer's Today tab shows the packet and the progress, and there's a **Reset Crew** badge (done by noon).

**Progressive discipline:** attendance and repeat performance problems go on file. Tardy: they clock in late, and aren't working or paid until they show. No-call no-show: they don't come in and don't call. Repeat mistake: a trained associate makes the same mistake again within a week (a rookie's mistakes are a training problem: coach them). Calling out sick with notice is not discipline. The Crew tab shows each person's step and open problems (⏰ 🚫 🔁), and **✎** opens the corrective-action form. The steps: documented verbal, written warning, final written warning, termination. Each step needs a new problem since the last one (two tardies, or one repeat mistake), a no-call no-show goes straight to a final, and a second one ends the job. Pick the step and what goes in the write-up: dates and times, the policy, the expectation, the next step and a signature. Leave out opinions, hearsay and comparisons. Skip a step and HR sends it back (a termination without a final isn't approved); write someone up with nothing on file and it looks like a grudge; go lighter than policy and it's inconsistent. A step makes new problems half as likely for 3 weeks. Leave a problem sitting for 5+ days and the rest of the crew loses morale. It's on the checklist when someone is due, and the weekly review grades it.

**District manager store walk:** every 10 to 16 days the district manager emails that they're coming, usually two days ahead, sometimes the same morning ("I'm in the area"). It's on your checklist. At that time the DM waits at the front of the store for 15 minutes; walk up and press **E** to walk the store together. They grade 8 areas as the store stands right then: entrance and floors (trash, spills, the mat on wet days), in-stock (every department 60%+), facing (85%+), price tags (no scan errors, price changes hung), endcaps and the ad, out-of-stock tags, go-backs and damages, and the backroom. You get a score, and every miss becomes a follow-up due by noon two days later. The DM checks them then and emails you either way. If you don't meet them, they walk it alone, take 10 points off and say so. Missed follow-ups cost customer satisfaction and show up in the weekly review.

**Vendor reps:** sales reps from the DSD companies (and the broker for the dry grocery brands) email you the day before to set up a visit, and it goes on your checklist. At that time the rep waits in the office for 40 minutes; walk up and press **E** to meet. Each visit they pitch two things and it's your call. Some help the store: building a display for product that's in the ad, a scan-back the vendor pays for (hang the tags), pulling their own out-of-code product for a credit, extra cases right before a storm or for the ad. Some only help them: taking another brand's facings (the planogram audit finds it, and the shelf gets scan errors), cutting in an item that isn't in the system (it won't scan), extra cases you can't sell (they go stale as shrink), and gifts for you (ethics policy: politely decline). Each answer tells you why. Standing a rep up gets a "sorry I missed you" email, and the weekly review notes missed visits, gifts and calls that went the vendor's way.

**Weekly P&L:** the store week runs Saturday to Friday. Friday night closes it, and Saturday morning Finance emails you that the P&L is on the office computer (**📒 P&L**). It shows sales, cost of goods, gross profit (30% margin), shrink split into spoiled/expired/short, damages, price errors, inventory adjustments and clearance markdowns (budget 2% of sales), hourly payroll against the labor target, your salary, card fees, supplies, and rent and utilities, each against budget, down to the bottom line: store contribution. Read it like a store manager and pick the line that cost the most against budget as next week's action plan (lost sales count at their margin, not the whole sale). The right pick is worth XP, and the weekly review notes a P&L that never got a plan. The last 8 weeks stay in a history table.

**Weather:** every day has weather (it follows the season: winter brings cold, snow and ice; summer brings heat waves and storms), and you see a 3-day forecast on the Today tab, the Forecast & Order screen and the huddle sheet. Tomorrow's forecast is usually right; further out, less so. Snow, ice and storms keep shoppers home that day, but the day before one is a **stock-up day**: more customers, and they buy water, milk and eggs, bread and canned goods, and paper. Heat waves sell water, drinks and frozen. The suggested order doesn't know about weather, so order up yourself on the truck that lands first; a weather alert email comes two days ahead of a big storm. On rain and snow days, water gets tracked in at the front door: press `E` at the front door to put the entrance mat and wet-floor signs out, or customer satisfaction drops every hour. Snow and ice also mean more call-outs (bad roads), and some DSD vendors cancel on big storm days.

**Customer interactions:** a few times a day a shopper stops and needs someone: a yellow **?** (or a red **!** if they're upset) with a patience bar over their head, and a toast tells you where. Walk up and press `E`, then pick what you say. "Where is...?": walk them to it (best), name the aisle (only if you're right), or "not my department" (never). "Any more in the back?": go check before you say no; if it's there, that's a sale you almost lost; if it's not, offer something similar. A price dispute: walk over and look at the tag together; if the tag is wrong, honor it and the tag gets fixed; "the register is always right" starts a fight. An upset customer: listen, apologize, fix it, thank them; excuses and "not my fault" make it worse. Afterward you see what happened and why. Nobody comes within about 7 minutes and they leave unhappy; a crew member working nearby steps in after a couple of minutes, as well as their skill allows.

**Performance reviews and raises:** every associate gets a review 14 days after they start (the probation review), then every 28 days. HR emails you the day it's due, the Crew tab shows **📝 Review**, and it's on the checklist. The review sheet shows the facts on file for four areas: Productivity (skill, rank, days worked), Accuracy (mistakes, training, coaching), Attendance (call-outs out of scheduled shifts) and Teamwork (arguments they started, personality). Rate each 1 to 5, then pick a raise from the merit matrix for the overall rating (under 2: 0% · 2–2.75: 1–2% · 2.75–3.5: 2–3% · 3.5–4.25: 3–4% · 4.25+: 4–5%; budget 3% on average). After you deliver it you see your ratings next to what the facts say. Rate below the facts and they feel cheated (morale drops); rate above them and the review is useless as documentation. A raise below the matrix costs morale, and a top performer may give notice; above the matrix eats the merit budget. Raises are permanent: they change the hourly wage and your labor cost. A review 3+ days late costs that person morale every day.

**Morning huddle:** on mornings you open, walk to the break room (top right of the backroom, marked 📣 HUDDLE while the window is open) between 5:30 and 8:00 AM and press `E` to pull the crew on the clock together for five minutes. The huddle sheet shows yesterday's numbers (sales vs goal, in-stock, when the truck finished, labor, grade) and today's plan (sales goal, truck size, DSD vendors, price tags, who's on, and anything special: a recall, a reset, the ad flip). Pick **one priority** for the day (the options are in no particular order; reading the day is the point). The crew makes half the mistakes in that kind of work all day, and if you picked the day's biggest issue (a recall beats everything, then a reset or the ad flip, then a big truck or a bad in-stock day), they get a morale lift from the clear direction. Add a **shout-out** to recognize someone in front of the team (worth more than a thank-you in the aisle). Skip it and nothing breaks, but the checklist and your weekly review notice.

**Hiring and interviews:** three new applicants every morning (office computer → Hiring). The card shows only the résumé: the job they want, years at their last job, the skill they *claim* (it can be a star high), their wage ask (based on that claim) and the availability on their application. Interview them first: up to 3 interviews a day, 3 questions each, so pick what you need to know. "Walk me through your last job" shows their real skill, "Why are you leaving?" shows their attendance (a red flag means more call-outs), "What days and hours can you really work?" shows their real availability (1 in 5 put "open" on the application and can't actually work most of the shifts they asked for), "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker" shows their personality (a hothead starts fights), and the customer question shows how they treat shoppers. Some questions on the list are illegal: age, kids or family, religion, where someone is from. Ask one and the applicant may walk out, HR emails you, and it shows on your review. You can still hire on the spot from the résumé; you find out the rest after they start.

**Store music:** futuristic ambient in-store radio plays in the background, generated live like the sound effects (no audio files): warm synth pads on smooth jazz-style chords, a soft electric pluck with echo, a round bass, glassy chimes and brushed hi-hats. It never repeats exactly. Four brighter day tracks and two slower, darker overnight tracks; it changes tracks every few minutes and shows "Now playing". 🎵 in the top bar turns the music on or off (🔊 still mutes everything). It pauses when you switch away from the game.

**Interview scene:** an interview pauses the game and opens a sit-down scene in the office: the applicant across the desk in pixel art, a dialogue box with their answer, and your questions as buttons. Watch their face and body language as well as their words: excited, nervous, confident, defensive, uncomfortable, or "something's off" when the answer doesn't match the truth (an oversold résumé shows when you ask about their last job). A line under each answer describes what they do ("Glances away right before answering"). When the questions are used up, hire them right there or thank them and finish. ✕ steps out and you can come back with Continue.

**Availability and time off:** every associate has availability: the days and hours they can work (open, a student who can only work after 2 PM on weekdays, a parent who has to be done by 5 PM, no Sundays, a second job on two weekdays). It shows in Crew, Hiring and the Schedule. Schedule someone outside it and they don't come in. Now and then someone's availability changes, starting two weeks out (they email you, and the Schedule shows it). Associates also ask for days off, 1 to 3 weeks out, by email; answer them in 🗓 Schedule → Time-off requests before that week's schedule posts (Thursday 5 PM the week before; you always get at least 2 days). **Approve** and they're off that day (the cell goes Off) and you cover the hours. **Deny** and they're unhappy (more if it mattered to them, like a wedding or a funeral), and some call in "sick" that day anyway. No answer in time counts as a no, only worse. If you approve a day off and leave them on the schedule, they don't come in. The Schedule marks every cell that won't be worked in red, and posting with problems asks you first.

**Endcap displays (switched Tuesday):** every endcap is a real display that holds up to 6 cases of one department, built from that department's backstock. A stocked endcap lifts its department's sales (+12% each, 2 max) and some shoppers take product off it instead of the shelf. When it runs out it lifts nothing, shows **EMPTY**, and costs you customer satisfaction every hour (the DM notices too). A fill bar under each display shows how full it is: fill it from backstock (`E` at the endcap); a Replenishment associate and idle crew fill low displays, and you can order anyone to "Endcaps". The Display Planner on the office computer only *plans* the next set: on Monday, plan the ad departments' endcaps (the suggested order adds the cases to build them, so they come on Monday night's truck), and the switch happens on the floor on Tuesday from 6 PM until Wednesday noon (`E` at the endcap, or the closing crew does it). Tearing a display down sends what's left on it back to backstock; a new display with no backstock goes up short or empty. Anything not switched by Wednesday noon waits until next Tuesday. Taking a display down (planning it Empty) can happen any day.

**Backroom layout:** the store can't change shape, but you can rearrange the backroom (Office computer → 🧱 Backroom Layout). Move each department's float spot, reload staging (an 8×1 row or a 4×2 block) and the baler. Nothing can go on receiving, the dock, doorways or the backstock rack aisle, and there must be a path around everything. The tab shows walk distances (receiving → float → shelf, baler, reload staging, dock) against the original layout: shorter trips mean a faster truck.

**On your phone:** open https://therarefew-arch.github.io/GroceryPractice/ and turn the phone sideways. On touch screens you get a floating analog joystick: put your thumb down anywhere on the left side (anywhere under the game with the phone upright) and the stick starts right there; push further to go faster, and it follows your thumb if you drag past the edge. A quick tap there still taps the game. You also get a big **E** button (hold it to repeat), a blue **Q** button when there's a second action, **RUN** (tap to toggle), zoom, pause and full screen. You can also tap the yellow action text at the bottom, and tap a crew member to see their card. To install it like an app: iPhone, Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen; Android, Chrome → ⋮ → Add to Home screen / Install app. It works offline after the first load. Your phone keeps its own save. Add `?touch=1` to the URL to force the touch controls on (or `?touch=0` to turn them off).

**Crew orders (micromanage):** click (or tap) an associate on the map, or use 📌 Orders in the Crew tab, and tell them exactly what to do: work the truck, break down pallets, unload the trailer, get one department's freight out, fill holes in a department, run an aisle's bird, face an aisle, cycle count a department, work reclaim, work the go-backs, watch the back door for DSD, hang price tags (and "temporarily out" tags), pull a recall, work the planogram reset, switch or fill an endcap, pick up cardboard or do backroom upkeep. They drop what they're doing, work the order until it's done or about 2 hours pass, then go back to their job. Orders outside their job (dashed buttons) work 15% slower unless they're a Lead or Superstar. More than 4 orders to one person in a day starts to cost morale: they want to finish something.

**Weekly DM review:** after Friday's close (weeks run Saturday to Friday) your District Manager grades the week. He is harsh but fair: for every problem he tells you what he saw, why it matters and what to do next week. Reviews also land in your email and on the Records tab.

**Records board:** Office computer → 🏆 Records tracks your personal bests (best score, top sales, fastest truck, lowest labor %, best in-stock, lowest OOS count, longest B-or-better streak, most coaching in a day).

**Smoother handling:** you move and work at the game speed you pick (2× and 4× speed you up too). One `E` grabs up to 3 cases of the same department off a pallet; hold `E` to keep picking, sorting and stocking. Whatever `E` will act on gets yellow corner brackets, and the thing you're facing wins when two are close. You slide around wall corners instead of catching on them, and floats and birds trail smoothly in front of you.

**Realism hooks:** cold pallets spoil if they sit in the back · forecasts use sales *plus* lost sales · endcaps lift demand and feed the forecast · vendors give up after 45 minutes · open price tags cause overcharges and complaints.
